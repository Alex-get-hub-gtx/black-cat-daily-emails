Your texts are doing the opposite

Fast is not warm.

Most women reply fast because they want him to know they're interested.

It's well intentioned and it's the single most damaging habit in modern dating.

Here's the mechanism.

Response time is a status signal, and it's read unconsciously by everyone, including you. You already know this. Think about how it feels when someone replies to you instantly every single time versus someone whose reply you're slightly pleased to see arrive.

Reply speed communicates one thing: how much of your attention was already pointed at him.

An instant reply, every time, tells him your phone was in your hand and his name was what you were waiting for. That's not warmth. That's availability, and availability is priced accordingly.

And here's the part that stings. It doesn't matter how brilliant the message is. The speed arrives first. He's already assessed it before he's read a word.

Now, I'm not telling you to time your replies. I said yesterday what happens when you do that from an anxious place. It leaks.

What I'm telling you is to stop living in your phone, and let the natural result be slower replies.

There's also a length thing, and this one is bigger than people think.

Match or come in under. If he writes one line, you write one line. If he writes a paragraph, you can write a paragraph. The moment your messages are consistently three times longer than his, you've shown him the balance of investment and he will adjust to it. Men do not read long messages from women as enthusiasm. They read them as weight.

Here's the scenario.

He sends "hey how was your day." She sends back four hundred words. Every time. She thinks she's being open.

He's receiving a daily report from someone who clearly has nothing else going on, and he starts replying with one line, and she writes four hundred words about why he's being distant.

What actually works.

Leave your phone in another room for blocks of the day. Not as a tactic. Then the slowness is real and it carries no anxiety in it.

Match his length. Under, never over.

End first sometimes. "Right, I'm off, speak later" sent by you, while the conversation is still good. He'll feel it.

And answer the question he asked. Not the question plus three more things plus how you feel about it.

Fast replies do not say I like you. They say I was waiting.

Your one move today.

One thing: put your phone in another room for two hours tonight. That's it.

You'll reply late, naturally, with nothing performed. That's the version that works.

How many times a day do you check whether he's replied? Give me the real number. Reply, nobody is counting but you.
