How he treats a waiter is a warning

Five years early.

Most women notice it and let it go.

He was a bit short with the waiter. A bit curt on the phone to the call centre. Slightly dismissive about the cleaner. And he was perfectly lovely to her all evening, so she files it under stress.

Here's why that was the most important thing that happened at dinner.

How a man treats someone who cannot affect his outcome is who he is when you can no longer affect his outcome.

Here's the mechanism. Behaviour toward people with power over us is managed. Behaviour toward people with no power over us is unmanaged, and unmanaged behaviour is the real thing.

The waiter cannot help him, promote him, leave him, or damage him. So there's no reason to perform. What you're watching is his actual default setting with another human being, running with the brakes off.

And here's why it predicts your future specifically.

Right now, you have enormous power over his outcome. You're new, you might leave, he wants you. So he's managing. Every man manages at month two.

In year five, you will have considerably less power. You'll be committed, possibly living together, possibly with children, possibly earning less because of it. Your ability to simply leave will be lower, and he will know it.

At that point, his management of his behaviour toward you becomes optional. And what surfaces is the default setting. The one you saw at dinner.

This is not a theory about bad men. It's a theory about what happens to anyone's behaviour when the incentive to perform is removed.

Here's what to watch for, beyond rudeness.

Contempt rather than anger. Anger is loud and it passes. Contempt is a small facial expression and a tone, and it's the single most reliable predictor of a relationship ending that researchers have found. Look for the eye roll, the little scoff, the sigh.

How he talks about service staff after they've gone. The comment in the car.

How he is with his mother. Not whether he loves her. Whether he's dismissive with her, and whether she defers to him in a way that looks established.

And whether his warmth is rank-dependent. Watch him move through a room. Does the temperature change based on who he's talking to? That's the tell.

What actually works.

Take him somewhere with service in the first three dates. Deliberately. A restaurant, not a bar. You need to see it.

Treat contempt as disqualifying, even when it's not aimed at you. Especially when it's not aimed at you.

And stop explaining it for him. "He's just tired" is you doing the work of hiding his character from yourself.

You are not watching how he treats a waiter. You are watching a preview of how he treats you once you can't easily leave.

Your one move today.

Think back. What did the last one do with people who couldn't do anything for him?

You'll remember something. You noticed it at the time.

Have you ever ignored this one? Reply and tell me what happened later. I suspect the stories line up.
