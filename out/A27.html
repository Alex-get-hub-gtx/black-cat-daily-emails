The difference between love and relief

One of them is a trap.

Most women have had this moment and called it love.

Three days of silence. The stomach ache, the checking, the certainty it's over. And then his name on the screen, and the flood of warmth through the whole body, and the thought: God, I love him.

That wasn't love. That was relief. And your brain files relief in the same drawer as joy.

Here's the mechanism.

Relief is the removal of pain. And the removal of pain produces a rush that is chemically close to pleasure, close enough that you cannot tell them apart from the inside.

The important part is what relief requires. It requires the pain first. No pain, no relief. Which means a relationship that runs on relief must keep producing distress in order to keep producing the high.

And that is the exact architecture of every relationship you could not leave.

He pulls away, you suffer. He returns, you flood. You label the flood love. The flood was real. The love is unproven. All you've actually established is that he can reliably cause and then end your distress, which is not a quality. It's a position.

Here's how to tell what you're in.

Ask yourself: do I feel good with him, or do I feel good when the bad feeling stops?

Those produce completely different relationships. The first one is a baseline you live at. The second one is a cycle you ride.

And there's a simpler test. Think about the last ten strong feelings you've had about him, and count how many happened during a period when everything was fine.

If almost all of your big feelings cluster around ruptures and repairs, around the pulling away and the coming back, then what you have is not a love story. It's a pattern with two moving parts, and you are only one of them.

This is also why the steady man feels flat. He never creates the pain, so he never delivers the relief, so you never get the flood. You read the absence of the flood as the absence of love. It's the absence of a wound.

What actually works.

Measure the baseline, not the peaks. How do you feel on an ordinary Tuesday when nothing is happening? That number is the relationship. The peaks are weather.

Notice the label at the moment it happens. When the relief hits, say the word in your head. Relief. Not love. Relief. Naming it accurately once is worth more than a month of analysis.

And stop treating the return as evidence. He came back. That tells you he came back.

If the best feeling you ever get from him is the one where the hurting stops, he is not the source of the good feeling. He's the source of the hurting.

Your one move today.

Rate today, with no drama in it, out of ten. Just today. How good does this actually feel on a normal day?

That's your real number. Write it down somewhere you'll find it in a month.

What's your baseline number? Reply with it, just the number if you like.
