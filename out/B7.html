Why you feel worse after you win

The pursuit was the reward.

He came back. He's doing everything you wanted. He's making plans, he's texting first, he's clearly invested.

And you feel flat. Possibly worse than flat. And you feel like a monster for it, because this is what you spent eight months wanting.

Here's the mechanism, and knowing it is a real relief.

The chemical that drives wanting peaks in anticipation, not in attainment.

Dopamine is not the reward chemical. It's the pursuit chemical. It fires hardest when a reward is uncertain and approaching, and it drops away once the thing is secured. Which means the best feeling in the entire sequence occurs before you get it.

So the eight months of wanting him were, chemically, the high. Not the getting.

This is one of the most disorientating experiences in dating and almost nobody has language for it. You spend months in a state of intense longing, you assume that intensity is love, and when the uncertainty resolves, the intensity goes with it, and you conclude you've stopped loving him.

You haven't. You've stopped chasing him. Those feel identical from the inside and they're not the same thing at all.

Here's how to tell what you're actually dealing with, because there are two possibilities and they require opposite responses.

One: the flatness is withdrawal, and underneath it there's a man you genuinely like. Give it six weeks. Real attachment builds slowly and quietly and it doesn't announce itself with fireworks. The feeling you're waiting for is not coming back in that form, and what replaces it is better and much less exciting.

Two: the chase was the whole thing, and with the uncertainty removed there's nobody there you actually want. That's also a real answer. Some men are only interesting while they're ambivalent, and the moment they commit you can see clearly what you were ignoring.

The way to tell is to ask what you think about when you're not thinking about the relationship status. Do you enjoy his company? Is he someone you'd pick as a friend? Is the ordinary Tuesday any good?

If the Tuesday is good and the feeling is flat, that's withdrawal and it passes.

If the Tuesday was never good and you only wanted him when he was uncertain, that's your answer and it's worth taking seriously rather than manufacturing more drama to get the feeling back.

What actually works.

Don't create uncertainty to feel something. This is the trap, and women do it constantly at this exact point. Picking a fight to get the intensity back is how a good relationship gets dismantled by someone who thinks she's bored.

Give it six weeks of ordinary before you decide.

And learn what calm feels like. If every relationship you've had ran on activation, calm will register as nothing for a while. That's a measurement problem, not a verdict.

The eight months of wanting him was the drug. What you have now is the thing, and the thing is quieter than the wanting was.

Your one move today.

Rate the Tuesday. Not the drama, the ordinary day. Out of ten.

That number is the relationship, and it's the only one worth looking at.
