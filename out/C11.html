How to evaluate a man you could marry

None of them are chemistry.

Chemistry tells you almost nothing about the next thirty years. Here's what does.

How he behaves when he's wrong. Does he get curious or does he get cold? This is the single most predictive thing on the list, because you will be in disagreement for thirty years and his behaviour in those moments is the texture of your life.

Whether he can be disagreed with by someone below him. His junior, a waiter, a younger relative. Disagreement he can afford to lose.

What he does when he's disappointed. Not angry, disappointed. Does it come out sideways, days later, as coldness? Sulking at forty is the same as sulking at sixty.

Whether he has friends who'll tell him the truth. A man surrounded by people who agree with him has no correction mechanism and you will become the only one, which is an unbearable job.

His relationship with his mother, specifically whether he's dismissive of her. Not whether they're close. Whether there's contempt.

Whether he does small things he said he'd do. Still the fastest test there is.

How he talks about his exes. Not whether it's positive. Whether every one of them was crazy. If all of them were, there's one constant.

Whether he's ever changed his mind about something important, and can say so.

What he's like with money when it's tight, if you've seen it. Generosity when flush is not information.

Whether he's building something. Not success, direction.

And whether you're a better version of yourself around him. This one is yours, not his, and it's the most important. Some men make you smaller in ways you only notice later.

Here's what's not on the list, and deliberately.

How he makes you feel in the first three months. How attractive he is. How well you get on. How much you have in common. Whether he's romantic. All lovely and none of them predictive.

And here's how to use it.

Not as a checklist to run on a date. Most of these can't be answered in the first month and shouldn't be. They're things to watch for across a year, and the first time he's wrong, or disappointed, or skint, is the single most informative day in the relationship.

What actually works.

Watch for the first disagreement rather than avoiding it. It's the most useful thing that will happen in the first three months.

Note the small promises from week one.

And ask yourself the last one honestly, every few months. Am I a better version of myself here?

You do not need to interview him. You need to be present on the ordinary days when something goes slightly wrong, because that is when he tells you everything.

Your one move today.

Pick the three from the list you've never actually checked for.

Those are your blind spots, and they'll be the same three every time.

Which one have you ignored before? Reply and tell me what it cost.
