Money, power, and who decides

Stated plainly, once.

In a serious relationship, money is not a practical subject. It's the structure that determines who gets to make decisions, and most couples never discuss it, so the structure forms by accident and always in the same direction.

Here's the mechanism.

Decision-making power tracks financial independence, not income, and the two are different.

A woman earning well who has merged everything has less independence than a woman earning modestly with her own account and her own savings. It isn't the amount. It's whether leaving is survivable, and whether day to day spending requires anyone's agreement.

And this doesn't announce itself. It shows up as small things. Whether you'd buy something without mentioning it. Whether a big purchase is a joint decision or his decision that you're consulted on. Whose career moves when there's a conflict.

Those are all downstream of an arrangement nobody designed.

Here's the architecture that works, and it's simple.

Three pots. Yours, his, and joint.

Joint covers shared costs, proportional to income. That's the fair bit: if he earns twice what you do, he contributes twice as much, because equal contribution from unequal incomes is not equality, it just means the lower earner has no money left.

And yours is genuinely yours. Not an allowance, not accounted for, not discussed. The same for him. Every adult should have money they don't have to explain.

The three pot structure is boring and it prevents more resentment than any amount of communication.

Then the three conversations that actually matter.

What happens if one of us stops earning. Illness, redundancy, children. Decide it in advance, in principle, while it's hypothetical and nobody's frightened.

Whose career moves. If there's a conflict, what's the rule? Most couples never decide and so it defaults to whoever earns more, which compounds, which is how it ends up never being hers.

And what's protected. Pension, savings, anything in your own name. Especially if you're the one who's going to take time out.

Here's the part that's unglamorous and matters most.

If you take time out of work, your pension stops and your earning power decays, and that gap is permanent. A couple who decide together that his pension contributions will cover both of them, or that she's compensated from joint money, have solved something that otherwise quietly costs one person a decade of security.

Nobody has that conversation. It takes about ten minutes.

What actually works.

Three pots, proportional contribution, and your own money that nobody audits.

Decide the what-ifs while they're hypothetical.

And protect your own name on something, always.

Money in a relationship is not about fairness. It's about who can afford to disagree, and that gets decided by an arrangement most couples never actually make.

Your one move today.

Which of the three conversations have you never had?

Pick one and have it this month. Four sentences.

Do you have money nobody audits? Reply yes or no.
