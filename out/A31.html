How to text a man

The whole system, in one email.

Most women think texting is just talking with a keyboard.

It isn't. Texting is the one place where every single thing I teach becomes visible and measurable, and it's where most women give the whole game away before they've met for the second time.

Here's the entire system. Five rules.

One. Match his length, or come in under.

If he writes a line, you write a line. The moment your messages are consistently longer than his, you've published the balance of investment. Men do not read long messages as enthusiasm. They read them as weight, and weight makes a man answer slower, which makes you write more, which is a spiral with a known ending.

Two. Let your reply time be a side effect, not a strategy.

Don't count minutes. Put the phone down and live, and the timing sorts itself out. The reason timed delays fail is that an anxious woman waiting forty minutes still sends an anxious message at minute forty one. Change the life, not the clock.

Three. End it first, while it's good.

This is the one nobody does and it's the most powerful. When the conversation is flowing nicely, that's when you say "right, I'm off out, talk later" and go. You've just left him on an upswing. The last thing he has of you is wanting more, and that's what he'll carry around all evening.

Most women ride the conversation until it dies of natural causes, and then the last thing he has of you is a thread that fizzled.

Four. Leave the end off.

"Had the maddest weekend" is better than the full story. Mention the thing and move on. If he's interested he asks, and his asking is him investing, which is the entire point of the exchange.

Five. Answer the question he asked. Nothing else.

He asks how your day was. Answer that. Not that plus your sister plus how you're feeling about work plus a question about his mum. One thing. Men process one thing at a time and a wall of text gets a one word reply, every time.

Now the two repairs you'll actually need.

The dead thread. You haven't spoken in a week and you want to. Don't open with "hey stranger" and don't open with an apology. Open with something that happened: "Just saw a man on the tube reading your book." Specific, light, no question required, no reference to the gap.

The dry reply. He's gone one word on you. Do not fill it. Match it, or send nothing. A one word answer met with a paragraph teaches him he can spend one word and receive five hundred.

Your texts are not where you build the attraction. They're where you either protect it or spend it.

Your one move today.

End the next conversation first. While it's still good. Mid-flow.

You'll hate doing it. He'll think about you for three hours.

Which of the five is hardest for you? Reply with the number. I'd bet money it's three.

See you tomorrow!
