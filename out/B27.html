How to stop auditing yourself

Performing it is still performing.

Here's a problem my own work creates, and I'd rather name it than pretend it doesn't happen.

You learn all of this, and then you can't stop watching yourself.

Was that reply too fast. Was that too much information. Should I have said that. Did I just over-explain. Am I being the golden retriever right now.

And the anxiety you had about him has been replaced by anxiety about your own performance. Which is, honestly, worse, because now it's running all the time and it doesn't even need him present.

Here's the mechanism.

Self-monitoring is cognitively expensive and it leaks. A woman running a checklist in her head is not actually present, and absence of presence reads exactly like nervousness, because it is nervousness.

So the monitoring produces the thing it's trying to prevent.

And it tells you something important, which is that you've been treating this as a set of behaviours to execute rather than what it actually is, which is a structural change in your life.

Here's the test for which one you're doing.

If you have to remember the rules, you're performing.

If you're busy, and your week is full, and you can settle your own nervous system, then you don't reply instantly because you weren't looking at your phone, not because you remembered not to. Nothing is being executed.

That's the difference between a woman doing the method and a woman being the thing the method describes.

So when you catch yourself auditing, the answer is almost never to try harder at the behaviour. It's to go and look at the structure, because the auditing is a symptom of the structure not being there yet.

What actually works.

Go back to the pillars. Every behaviour I teach is downstream. If you're having to remember them, the upstream work is incomplete, and the fastest route out of self-monitoring is a fuller week.

Pick one behaviour for a month, not nine. One. "I don't reply after 10pm." Hold that, ignore everything else. A single rule becomes a habit in about four weeks and then stops costing anything. Nine rules stay expensive forever.

Let the imperfect ones go unexamined. You will text too fast sometimes. It costs almost nothing. The reviewing of it costs much more.

And notice when you're doing it with people who aren't him. If you're auditing yourself with friends, with colleagues, in a shop, this isn't about dating any more and the pillars conversation is the one that matters.

You are not trying to perform a magnetic woman. You're trying to build a life where that behaviour is just what happens, and the checklist is a sign you're not there yet.

Your one move today.

Pick one rule. Just one. Drop the rest for a month.

The relief is immediate and it's the point.
