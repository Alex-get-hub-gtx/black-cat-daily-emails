Confidence isn't a decision.

Most women think confidence is something you decide to have.

So she decides. She walks in telling herself she's the prize, she's done the affirmations, she's wearing the dress. And forty minutes later she's checking her phone under the table and she cannot work out why the energy slipped.

Here's why. You cannot decide your way out of a nervous system state.

Confidence is not a thought. It's a physiological condition, and the body publishes it whether you agree or not.

When your system is activated, when there's adrenaline and cortisol running, your body does a specific set of things and it does them without asking you. Your breath moves up into your chest and gets shallower. Your jaw tightens. Your blink rate goes up. Your voice rises in pitch and speeds up. Your hands need something to hold. You laugh a quarter second too early.

He's not analysing any of this consciously. He doesn't need to. Humans read nervous system state in each other instantly, because for most of our history it was the fastest way to know if there was something to be afraid of.

So the woman who has decided to be confident while her body is running an anxiety programme is sending two messages, and only one of them arrives.

This is the thing I wish someone had told me at twenty two. You are not failing at confidence. You are trying to install it at the wrong layer.

Here's what the two look like from across the table.

Activated: she fills silences. She agrees too fast. Her sentences go up at the end. She touches her hair, her glass, her phone. She laughs at things that weren't jokes. Her shoulders are a centimetre higher than they should be.

Settled: she lets a pause sit. Her voice drops at the end of sentences. Her hands are still. She takes a breath before she answers. She's slower than the room.

The second woman has not had better thoughts. She has a different body.

What actually works.

Breathe into your belly and make the exhale longer than the inhale. Four in, six or eight out. Do it for ninety seconds before you walk in anywhere. The long exhale is the direct line to the part of the nervous system that makes you calm, and it's the only lever that works in under two minutes.

Drop your voice at the end of sentences. Not deeper overall, just down at the end instead of up. Up at the end is a question, and a woman whose statements sound like questions is asking permission all evening.

Unclench your jaw and let your tongue rest on the floor of your mouth. Sounds absurd. It drops tension through your whole face and he will read it as warmth.

He is not responding to what you believe about yourself. He's responding to what your body is doing about it.

Your one move today.

Before the next thing that makes you nervous, four in, eight out, five times. That's ninety seconds.

You'll feel your shoulders drop on the third one. That's the whole trick, and it's free.
