How to leave a party

The most underrated move there is.

Most women leave when the party's over.

She stays until the energy drops, until the good people have gone, until she's tired and slightly flat, and then she gets a taxi home at two.

Here's the move that changes how an entire room remembers you.

Leave while it's still brilliant.

Everything I said about ending a conversation first applies tenfold to a room. The peak and the end are what get stored, and if you leave at the peak then the peak and the end are the same moment.

Which means the last thing everybody in that room has of you is you being wonderful and then being gone. Not you being tired at one in the morning. Not you being the last one there.

And then, this is the bit, the room talks about you after you've left. That's when it happens. A woman who leaves at eleven when it's still buzzing becomes a small event. "Where did Anna go?" Someone always asks. Nobody has ever asked where the woman who stayed till the end went.

Here's the mechanism underneath it.

Presence has diminishing returns. The first hour of you in a room is the most valuable hour. By hour four you've been fully absorbed, everything interesting has been said, and your scarcity has gone to zero. You're furniture with a drink.

Scarcity is not a trick. It's arithmetic. The same woman is worth more at hour one than at hour five, and the only variable is how long she stayed.

And for the man in the room who was watching you, the woman who leaves early is an open loop. He didn't get to the end of the evening with you. He'll be thinking about that in the taxi.

What actually works.

Decide your exit time before you arrive. Then leave at it, regardless of how good it is. Especially if it's good. If you're deciding in the moment you'll always stay, because the moment is enjoyable, which is exactly why the rule has to be set in advance.

Leave without a tour. No round of goodbyes, no announcement, no "right everyone, I'm off." Say goodbye to two people and go. A visible departure is a performance. A quiet one is a disappearance, and disappearances get noticed more.

Don't explain why you're going. "I'm off, this was lovely" and nothing else. The second you give a reason, someone will solve it.

And never be the one who turns the lights on. Just, never.

The woman everyone remembers is not the one who was there the longest. It's the one who left while they still wanted her there.

Your one move today.

Next time you're out, set a time before you go and honour it.

Then notice, the next day, how many people mention that you left.

Are you a stayer or a leaver? Reply, I was a stayer for years.

See you tomorrow!
– Anna
