Why long term couples stop wanting each other

Cohabitation removes the only ingredient.

Everyone says passion naturally fades. It's presented as a law, like gravity.

It isn't a law. It's a predictable consequence of an arrangement nobody examines.

Here's the mechanism, and it's uncomfortable because the thing causing it is the thing everyone wants.

Desire requires distance. Intimacy removes distance. So the more successfully you merge, the less there is to want.

Think about what desire actually is. It's a reaching toward something separate from you. You cannot want what you already fully have and fully know and see at close range every single day.

Now think about what a shared life does. Same house, same bed, same routine, same bathroom, same weekend. You become, functionally, one organism with two bodies. Which is lovely, and secure, and the opposite of erotic.

This is not a failure of the relationship. It's the relationship working exactly as designed, and then being surprised by the side effect.

And here's why it hits women particularly hard. Desire in women is more responsive to context than to frequency of opportunity. It needs a sense of the other person as separate, slightly unknown, worth approaching. A man who is permanently available and completely familiar does not produce that condition, no matter how much you love him.

So the fix is not more time together. It's the opposite, and it feels wrong, which is why almost nobody does it.

What actually works.

Separateness on purpose. Nights apart. Trips without each other. Your own friends, your own evenings, your own interests. Not because the relationship is in trouble. Because distance is the raw material and you have to manufacture it once it stops occurring naturally.

See him from the outside. Watch him do the thing he's good at, around other people, where he isn't your partner but a man who is competent at something. This is one of the fastest routes back and it costs nothing.

Stop the full disclosure of the domestic. There are some things that, once shared, cannot be unshared. Everyone decides their own line. Just know that the line exists and that erasing it entirely has a cost.

And protect the difference between partner and co-manager. The logistics conversation expands to fill all available time if you let it. A couple who only ever talk about the house, the money and the schedule have become colleagues.

Here's the honest part. Some of this is a trade. Deep security and high desire pull in opposite directions, and most people choose security, and that's a legitimate choice.

But it should be a choice, not something that happens to you while you assume it's inevitable.

Passion does not fade on its own. It gets squeezed out by proximity, and proximity is the thing you both worked very hard to achieve.

Your one move today.

Put one night apart in the diary this month. Not a row, not a crisis. A night.

Then notice what comes back.

Have you felt this one? Reply, it's the one nobody talks about honestly.
