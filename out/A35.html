How to be interesting without performing

It's what you ask, not what you say.

Most women think being interesting means having good material.

So she arrives with stories. The travel, the funny thing at work, the opinions she's prepared. She performs, competently, and the evening is fine, and nothing happens.

Here's what actually makes a person feel that a conversation was extraordinary.

How much of themselves they revealed in it.

People do not walk away thinking "she was fascinating." They walk away thinking "I've never told anyone that." And then they attach that feeling to you.

Which means your job is not to be interesting. Your job is to be the person in front of whom he became interesting.

This is a skill and it has a shape. I call it the ladder.

Level one is facts. "What do you do?" Everyone asks this and the answer tells you nothing. Fine as a doorway, useless as a destination.

Level two is why. "Why that?" This is where it starts. Most people have never been asked why they do the thing they do, and they'll be slightly surprised and then they'll think about it properly.

Level three is the feeling or the cost. "What's the part of it nobody sees?" "What did you give up for that?" "When did you know?" This is the level where a man tells you something he hasn't said out loud in a year.

You get to level three in about four exchanges, and you only need to get there once in an evening.

Here's what it looks like.

"What do you do?" He's an engineer. Level one, done.

"Why engineering?" Because he liked taking things apart as a kid, his dad was the same. Level two. Now there's something real on the table.

"What was he like?" And now he's telling you about his father, and the whole temperature of the evening has changed, and you have said eleven words.

Compare that to the version where she spends the same ten minutes telling him about her trip to Japan.

What actually works.

Ask why, not what. Make it your default. It's two letters longer and it goes four levels deeper.

Follow the thing he lit up about, not the thing you'd planned to ask. The lighting up is the signal. Everything else is admin.

Say less than you want to. When he finishes an answer, don't fill it with your matching story. Let there be a beat. The beat is where he decides to say the next, truer thing.

And never say "I love that too." I know you mean it kindly. What it does is close the topic and move it to you.

You are not memorable because of what he learned about you. You're memorable because of what he learned about himself.

Your one move today.

In your next conversation with anyone, ask why twice in a row. Just twice.

Watch how fast it goes somewhere real.

What's the best question anyone's ever asked you? Reply, I collect these.

See you tomorrow!
