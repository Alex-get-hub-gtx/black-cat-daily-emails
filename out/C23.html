The friend who is where you were

Teaching, not rescuing.

She's in it. The one you got out of. And you can see the whole thing, including the ending, and she cannot see any of it.

So you tell her. And it goes badly, and she gets defensive, and six weeks later she's still there and now she's not telling you things.

Here's the mechanism.

Insight delivered from outside is almost never accepted, because accepting it means accepting that you've been wrong for a long time, and nobody can afford that in the middle of it.

When you tell her what's happening, you're not giving her information. You're asking her to indict herself, and her mind will defend her instead. That's not stubbornness, it's the same protective function that kept you in yours for as long as it did.

Think about who finally got through to you. It almost certainly wasn't the friend who explained it clearly. You left when you were ready, and usually after something happened, and the explanations had been available for a year.

Here's what actually helps, and it's much less satisfying.

Stay in. The single most valuable thing is that she keeps telling you things. The moment you become someone she has to defend herself to, you lose access, and you're the only person who can see it clearly. Being told is worth more than being right.

Ask instead of tell. "What do you think that was about?" "How did that feel?" "What would you want to happen?" Questions let her arrive at it herself, and the thing she arrives at herself is the only thing that'll move her.

Say the true thing once, clearly, and then stop. Not a campaign. Once. "I think he's not going to change and I think you know that." Said kindly, once, and then never repeated. She'll remember it. She'll come back to it in four months. Repeating it just makes you someone to avoid.

Don't rescue the logistics. Don't manage her, don't do the hard things for her, don't talk to him. Rescuing removes the consequence, and the consequence is the only thing that educates.

And hold your own line about what you'll absorb. You can love her and not be available at 1am four nights a week. Endless absorption makes you the pressure valve that lets the situation continue.

Here's the hardest part.

She may not get out. Some people don't, or not for years. And you have to be able to love her through that without it being contingent on her taking your advice.

What actually works.

Ask more than you tell. Keep access above being right.

Say the true thing once, then let it sit.

Don't rescue, don't manage, don't absorb without limit.

And remember what it took for you, which was usually not someone explaining it.

You cannot give her the thing you learned. You can only be the person she's still talking to when she finally learns it herself.

Your one move today.

If you've got one, ask her a question instead of telling her something.

"What do you want to happen?" is usually the one.

Have you got a friend in it right now? Reply, I'd like to know how you're handling it.
