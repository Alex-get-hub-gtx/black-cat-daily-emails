When you have outgrown him

Love is not the question.

This is the one nobody wants to say out loud.

He hasn't done anything wrong. He's kind, he's loyal, he loves you. And something in you has moved and he hasn't, and you've been pretending not to notice for about a year.

Here's the thing that makes this so hard.

There's no grievance. Nothing to point at. And without a grievance, leaving feels like cruelty and staying feels like a slow erasure, so most women do neither and let another two years pass.

Here's what's actually happened.

You've changed and he hasn't, and relationships run on roughly matched trajectories rather than on matched feelings.

Two people don't need to be the same. They need to be moving at a similar pace in a compatible direction. When one person's world is expanding and the other's isn't, the gap doesn't stay constant. It widens, because growth compounds.

And the discomfort you feel is not ingratitude. It's accurate measurement.

Here's the question that settles it, and it's not "do I love him."

Do I want to become more like the person this relationship is turning me into?

Because every relationship is shaping you. It has a gravity and a ceiling and a set of things it makes normal. And the honest question is not about him at all, it's about who you'll be in five years of this.

If the answer is yes, then the gap is survivable and probably worth closing.

If the answer is no, you already know, and you've known for a while, and the knowing is what's been making you so tired.

Now, the fair part, because this cuts both ways.

Sometimes "I've outgrown him" is a story a woman tells because she's bored, or because she's in the flat part after the chase ended, or because growth has become its own kind of addiction. That's real and it's worth checking.

The test is whether you respect him. Boredom and outgrowing feel similar and they're distinguishable by one thing: respect. If you still fundamentally respect him and you're restless, that's a you problem and it's fixable. If the respect has quietly gone, that's the other thing, and respect does not come back.

What actually works.

Ask the five year question honestly, in writing, alone.

Check the respect. That's your instrument, not the love.

Give it one real attempt before you decide. Say what you want out loud, once, and see if he moves toward it. Some men will. Most women never actually asked.

And if the answer is no, leave well rather than slowly. The slow version is worse for both of you and it takes years.

The question was never whether you love him. It's whether you want to become the woman this life is making you, and you already know that answer.

Your one move today.

Write the five year version. Honestly. One paragraph.

You'll know by the second sentence.
