Admiration is not attraction

Two different circuits.

Most women read compliments as desire.

He tells her she's beautiful, that she's impressive, that she's the smartest person in the room. So she assumes the interest is there and then can't understand why nothing ever happens.

Here's the distinction.

Admiration makes a man watch you. Attraction makes a man move.

They're genuinely separate. A man can hold you in extremely high regard and feel no pull toward you at all, and he will not necessarily know the difference himself, which is why his words are so misleading.

Admiration is an assessment. It's cognitive. He has evaluated you and the evaluation is high. It produces respect, compliments, and a slightly formal warmth.

Attraction is an impulse. It produces behaviour. Specifically it produces approach, risk, and inconvenience.

So the test is not what he says about you. It's what he's prepared to do about it.

Here's where women get stuck for years.

The man who admires her is wonderful to be around. He's complimentary, he's interested in her work, he remembers things. He sends her articles. He tells his friends she's great.

And he never asks her out properly, or he does and the energy is strangely flat, and she spends eight months wondering what's missing.

What's missing is the impulse. And you cannot generate an impulse in someone by being more admirable. In fact admiration can actively suppress it, because a man who has placed you slightly above him often becomes careful, and careful is the opposite of the thing you want.

This is the whole reason that being impressive is not a dating strategy.

Here's how to tell them apart quickly.

Admiration sounds like: you're amazing, you're so smart, I don't know how you do it. It's about qualities. It's delivered calmly.

Attraction sounds like almost nothing, because he's too busy doing something. He's rearranged his evening. He's touched your arm. He's gone slightly quiet. He's made a plan and he's nervous about it.

The compliments come from the admirer. The nervousness comes from the man who wants you.

What actually works.

Weight behaviour over language, permanently. This is the same rule as everything else in my work and it applies here most of all.

Stop collecting admiration. It feels like progress and it's a different currency entirely. A man who thinks you're impressive is not on a path to wanting you.

Be slightly less impressive and slightly more present. Not dimmer. Warmer. A man approaches a woman he can imagine being easy with, not a woman he's assessing.

And when a man admires you without moving, let it go. Nothing is coming. That's not pessimism, it's just the mechanism.

A man who thinks you're extraordinary and does nothing about it has told you exactly how he feels. You just heard the compliment instead of the inaction.

Your one move today.

Think of a man who's told you how great you are and never made a move.

Stop waiting for that one. He was telling you the truth, just not the thing you heard.

Have you ever been admired by someone who never moved? Reply, I think this is more common than anyone admits.
