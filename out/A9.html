Being easy to love makes you easy to leave

You cannot miss what never goes.

Most women are quietly running one strategy: be so good that leaving would be insane.

No fights she started. No demands. No inconvenience. The reasonable one, always. The one he could never complain about.

And then he leaves, or he fades, and she's holding a spotless record wondering what else she could possibly have done.

Here's the thing she did wrong, and it's the only thing.

She made herself unlosable.

People do not act to gain. They act to avoid loss. Losing something hurts roughly twice as much as gaining the same thing feels good, which is why a man will fight harder to keep a woman he's losing than to win a woman he's gaining.

Loss aversion is the single most powerful motivator in human behaviour.

And it requires one thing to work. There has to be something losable.

If you have made yourself permanent, if you are structurally always going to be there, then there is no loss available to him. You have removed the only lever that actually moves people. He can relax completely, which he does, and relaxation is not love. It's just the absence of alarm.

This is why being "the one who'd never leave" is not the safe position. It's the invisible one.

Let me be precise about what I'm not saying.

I'm not saying threaten to leave. Threats are loud, and loud is weak. A woman who says "I'll walk" has just told him she hasn't.

I'm saying be a woman for whom leaving is actually possible. Not as a weapon. As a fact.

That's a life she could keep if he vanished. Her own money. Her own people. Her own weekends. Somewhere to go on a Tuesday that has nothing to do with him.

A man can feel that. He can always feel it. And a man who can feel that you'd survive him is a man who behaves completely differently.

What actually works.

Build the exit you never use. Money you don't need from him, friends who are yours, a life with your name on it. Not because you're planning to go. Because the possibility is the whole mechanism.

Let consequences exist. When something isn't acceptable, let there be an actual cost. Not a conversation about the cost. The cost.

And stop announcing your permanence. "I'm not going anywhere" is a sentence that sounds like devotion and functions like a guarantee. Guarantees get taken for granted. All of them.

A man does not treasure the woman who could never leave. He treasures the woman who could, and hasn't.

Your one move today.

Put one thing in your week that is yours and non-negotiable. A class, a standing dinner, a night. Then keep it, even when something better is offered.

That's not a boundary you have to explain. It's just a Tuesday he can't have.

What would your life look like next month if he wasn't in it? Be honest with yourself before you reply. Then reply, I'd like to hear it.

See you tomorrow!
