Being the standard other women copy

Social proof running the other way.

At some point this stops being about getting a man and becomes about something else, and most women are not prepared for it.

You become the one other women watch.

Here's what actually happens. Your friends start asking you what to do. Someone copies something about how you handle things. A woman you barely know says something that makes it clear she's been paying attention. You become, quietly, a reference point.

And three things follow, and they're worth knowing in advance.

Your options change completely. Preselection is the strongest social signal there is, and it runs through women. A woman that other women orbit is read as high status instantly and permanently. This is not a small effect. It changes who approaches you, and it changes what they're prepared to offer.

Some friendships will not survive it. The ones that were built on being equally stuck. When one person gets out, the arrangement breaks, and it usually breaks quietly and from the other side.

And you'll feel like a fraud. Constantly. Because you know exactly how recently you were the one asking, and you know the parts that are still hard, and being treated as someone who has it worked out while you're still working it out is deeply uncomfortable.

Here's what I'd want you to do with it.

Don't perform having arrived. The temptation is to become the finished article, because that's what people seem to want from you. It's a trap. It stops you being able to say you're struggling, which means you stop getting help, which means you stall.

Be honest at the top. The most useful thing a woman slightly ahead can do is say what's still hard. It's also the thing that makes her trustworthy rather than intimidating.

And use the position rather than apologising for it. Being the standard is leverage, socially and romantically. You don't have to be embarrassed about it.

Here's the part that I think matters most.

This is the point where the work stops being about men entirely. Everything you built, the pillars, the regulation, the ability to choose, was ostensibly for a relationship. And then you get far enough along and realise the relationship was always a side effect, and what you actually built was a life, and a position, and a self you'd have wanted anyway.

That's the real outcome and nobody sells it, because it doesn't fit in a headline.

What actually works.

Be honest about what's still hard. It keeps you learning and it keeps you human.

Let the friendships that can't survive it go, without guilt.

And notice that the thing you were chasing turned out to be downstream of something else.

You stopped trying to be chosen and became the woman other women are studying. That's the actual destination, and it was never the one on the brochure.

Your one move today.

Name one woman who's watching you. Then tell her one true thing about what's still hard.

That's the whole move, and it does more than any advice you could give her.
