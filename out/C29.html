What I do when I feel myself slipping

I still do, by the way.

I should say this plainly, because I think it's the most useful thing I can give you.

I still slip.

Not often, and not for long, and not in the ways I used to. But the old pattern is not gone. It's quieter and further away and it's still there, and it comes back when I'm tired or when something lands on an old bruise.

Here's exactly what I do, because a protocol is more useful than reassurance.

I notice the physical first. It always shows up in my body before I know what's happening. A tightness, checking my phone without deciding to, a particular restlessness in the early evening. I've learned those three as early signals, and noticing them is most of the work.

I don't analyse it immediately. The instinct is to work out what it means and whether it's justified, and that analysis is the spiral, not the solution. Body first.

Four in, eight out. Five rounds. Every time. It's unglamorous and it's the thing that works.

Then I change my environment. Out of the room, out of the house if I can. Cold water. Movement. Twenty minutes of something physical. I don't try to think my way out of it because I know by now that I can't.

Then, and only then, I ask the question: is this about what's actually happening, or is this old?

Usually it's old. Not always. The honest version is that about a third of the time it's real information and I need to act on something, and the other two thirds it's a pattern reactivating.

And the test I use is the same one I gave you: has his behaviour actually been inconsistent? If no, it's mine. If yes, it's not, and I deal with it plainly rather than managing my feelings about it.

Then I tell one person. Out loud. Not a performance of being fine. The actual thing. This is the part I resisted longest and it's the one that shortens it most.

And then I go back to the structure. The week, the commitments, the thing I'm building. The structure holds me when the state doesn't, which is the entire reason the structure exists.

Here's what I want you to take from this.

The goal was never to become a woman who doesn't feel it. That woman doesn't exist, and the ones who appear to be her are usually suppressing, and it costs them.

The goal is a woman who recognises it early, has a protocol, and is back inside a day instead of inside three weeks.

That's it. That's the whole thing. It gets faster and it gets quieter and it doesn't disappear.

What actually works.

Know your three physical early signals. Everyone has them.

Body before analysis, every time.

Tell one person the real version.

And go back to the structure rather than waiting to feel better first.

I am not a woman who stopped feeling it. I'm a woman who knows what it is within about an hour, and that is the only difference between now and ten years ago.

Your one move today.

Name your three physical signals. The ones that show up before you know what's wrong.

Write them down. That's your early warning system and you've probably never named it.

What are your three? Reply and tell me. I'd really like to know.

See you tomorrow!
