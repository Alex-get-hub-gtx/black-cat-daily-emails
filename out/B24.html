How to ask and still be the prize

Expectation, not plea.

You've been told that asking for things makes you needy, so you've stopped asking for anything, and now you're quietly furious about a list of things you never mentioned.

Here's the correction.

Asking is not needy. The grammar you ask in is what makes it needy.

Same request, three different frames, three completely different outcomes.

The plea. "I know you're really busy and I feel bad even asking, but is there any chance you might be able to, only if it's not too much trouble..."

What he hears: this is a favour and she's braced for a no. So a no is clearly available, and he's being granted the role of someone doing her a kindness.

The complaint. "You never plan anything, it's always me."

What he hears: an accusation. Now he's defending rather than acting, and even if he does the thing, he's doing it to end an argument.

The expectation. "Book us something for Saturday. Surprise me."

What he hears: an instruction from a woman who assumes he's capable. There's no question mark, no apology, no accusation, and no exit.

And here's the thing almost nobody believes until they try it: the third one is the one men respond to best, and it's also the one that makes them feel best about themselves.

Because it carries an assumption of competence. You've asked him for something you clearly expect him to be good at.

The plea asks him to be generous. The complaint asks him to be less bad. Only the expectation asks him to be capable, and capable is the thing he actually wants to be.

Here's the structure.

Short. One sentence. Every additional clause is a leak.

No question mark where a full stop will do. "Book us something" rather than "could you maybe book something."

No reason. The moment you justify the request, you've made it contestable.

And then stop talking. Say it, and let it sit. Do not fill the pause with softening.

What actually works.

State it as a small instruction, warmly. The warmth is what stops it being cold. "Book us something Saturday, I want to be taken out." That's warm and it's not a request.

Then receive it well when he does it. Over-thanking turns it back into a favour. "That was perfect" and a real smile is the correct amount.

And ask for small things often, rather than big things rarely. Frequency builds the pattern. The pattern is the point.

Needy is not asking for things. Needy is apologising for asking, and then being grateful out of proportion when you get it.

Your one move today.

Ask for one small thing today in one sentence with no question mark.

Then say nothing after it.

What have you wanted and not asked for? Reply and tell me, I bet it's small.

See you tomorrow!
– Anna
