You want children and he is unsure

Unsure is an answer.

He's not against it. He's just not sure. He says maybe in a few years. He says he can see it one day.

And you've been holding that sentence for two years because it isn't a no.

Here's what I need you to hear.

For a man who is thirty five and has been asked directly, unsure is an answer. It's just not a final one, and the distinction matters less than you'd like.

Here's the mechanism. Men are not generally uncertain about this in the way women are led to believe. A man who wants children usually knows, and crucially, a man who wants children with you usually becomes certain about it, because the wanting attaches to a specific person and a specific life.

Which means "unsure" is often not uncertainty about children. It's uncertainty about this, or it's a genuine ambivalence about the whole structure of that life, and both of those have a way of resolving in the same direction after another three years of being asked.

There's a real version. Some men genuinely haven't thought about it and do arrive at certainty. The way to tell is whether he's actively thinking about it or just deferring it. A man who's genuinely working it out brings it up himself sometimes. A man who's deferring only ever discusses it when you raise it, and the answer is always a slightly different version of not yet.

Count who raises it. That's your test and it's very reliable.

Here's the part that's hardest.

You cannot wait for certainty and also keep your options. Those are mutually exclusive and every month you wait spends the thing you'd need if the answer turns out to be no.

So the deadline has to exist, it has to be private, and it has to be honoured. Everything I said about the private deadline applies here more than anywhere, because this is the one where the cost of a slipped deadline is not recoverable.

Pick the date. Make it real and reasonably soon, because two more years is a lot. Don't tell him. Live normally. And when it arrives, act.

And the thing not to do: do not try to persuade him. A man persuaded into fatherhood is a risk to you and to the child. You want a man who wants it, and wanting cannot be argued into existence.

What actually works.

Ask once, closed, calmly. Then count who raises it over the following months.

Set the private date and honour it.

Don't persuade, don't hint, don't leave articles around.

And get your own information in parallel, so that whatever you decide, you're deciding with facts.

He has not said no, which is why you've been able to stay. But a man who is still unsure after being asked twice has told you something, and you've been reading the grammar instead of the answer.

Your one move today.

Set the date. Write it somewhere only you see.

That single act will change how you feel this week, whatever happens later.

Are you waiting on an unsure? Reply with how long it's been.
