Grief after you get what you wanted

You lose the woman who was waiting.

Nobody warns you that getting it can make you sad.

You're in the relationship. It's good. And there's a grief sitting under it that makes no sense and that you can't mention to anyone, because how do you say you're mourning while your life is going well.

Here's what you're actually grieving, and it's real.

The woman who was waiting is gone, and she was somebody.

She had a project. She had a hope that organised her, and a story in which the good thing was still coming, and a version of herself that would exist once it arrived. That woman had years invested and she had a kind of integrity: she kept going, she didn't settle, she held out.

And then the thing arrives, and she's finished. Not rewarded. Finished. The hope resolves into a man who leaves towels on the floor, and the future-version-of-you that the hope was carrying quietly stops existing, because she's now the present version, and the present version is just a person.

That's a real loss and it deserves to be named.

There's a second layer, and it's heavier.

If the waiting was long, the waiting was also a story about why your life wasn't fully underway yet. And once the thing you were waiting for arrives, that explanation is gone, and everything else that isn't how you want it becomes visible with nothing to hide behind.

That's the part that produces the strange flatness after a win. It's not disappointment in him. It's the arrival of everything else.

And there's sometimes a third, which is grief for the years. The ones spent on men who were never going to. That grief usually can't come up while you're still in the fight, because it would stop you. It comes up when you're safe, which is why it arrives now, at the exact moment everything is finally alright.

What actually works.

Let it be grief rather than evidence. The single most dangerous move here is to read the sadness as proof that he's wrong. It isn't about him.

Say it out loud to someone. It's unsayable in most contexts, which is exactly why it sits there. A friend, a therapist, a page.

Build the next thing. The hope that was organising you needs somewhere to go, and if you don't give it a destination it will reattach to the relationship and start asking it to be more than it can be. This is the most practical thing on the list.

And grieve the years properly. Once, deliberately. They were real and they cost you something and pretending otherwise keeps it live.

You are not sad because it's wrong. You're sad because the woman who spent six years waiting has just been made redundant, and nobody thanked her.

Your one move today.

Write one paragraph to the woman who was waiting.

Say what she did well. Then let her go.

Have you felt this one? Reply, if you can put it into words. Very few people can.

See you tomorrow!
