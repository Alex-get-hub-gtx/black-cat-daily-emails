When he is in a bad season

Support without absorbing.

He's lost the job, or his father is ill, or something has gone badly wrong, and he's not himself.

And every instinct you have says: carry him. Take it off him. Manage everything until he's back.

Here's the distinction that decides whether your relationship survives his bad season.

Support holds the structure steady. Absorbing takes the weight off him entirely, and a man who has been fully relieved of his own weight does not recover faster. He recovers slower, and he resents you for it afterwards, and nobody can explain why.

Here's the mechanism.

A man's sense of himself runs on competence. In a bad season, competence is exactly what's collapsed. He feels useless, and he's watching himself fail at something.

Now, if you step in and run his life, you've confirmed the thing he's most afraid of. You've become evidence. Every meal you cook and every call you make on his behalf is, to the part of him that's struggling, proof that he cannot do it.

He will be grateful. He'll also start avoiding you, and he won't know why, and he'll say something like "I just need space."

Meanwhile you've done nothing but love him, which is what makes this so cruel.

Here's what actually helps.

Be steady rather than helpful. The single most valuable thing in his bad season is that you are unchanged. Same warmth, same life, same standards. A woman whose state didn't move is proof that the world hasn't ended.

Let him keep his jobs. Whatever he was doing before, he keeps doing, even badly, even slowly. Competence is rebuilt through doing, and taking the tasks away removes the only route back.

Do specific things, not general ones. "I'll handle dinner Thursday" is support. "Let me take care of everything" is absorption. One is a contribution, the other is a takeover.

Don't make it the only subject. He's a man having a bad time, not a project. Talk about other things. Go out. Laugh at something. The relief of fifteen minutes of normal is enormous.

And keep your own pillars up. This is the part women abandon first and it's the one that matters most, because if you dismantle your life to attend to his crisis, you'll both be on the floor and there'll be nothing to climb back toward.

Here's the hard one.

There's a difference between a bad season and a man who has decided you'll carry him permanently. A season has an end and visible effort inside it. If it's been a year and there's no effort, that's not a season, that's the arrangement, and the whole of this email doesn't apply.

What actually works.

Stay the same, deliberately.

Offer specific, bounded help. Then stop.

And don't take over the things he can still do badly.

He does not need you to carry it. He needs one thing in his life that didn't collapse when everything else did, and the most useful thing you can be is that.

Your one move today.

If you're in one, name one thing you've taken over that he could still do.

Give it back this week, without making it a conversation.

Have you ever carried someone through one? Reply, I'd like to hear how it went.
