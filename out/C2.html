The same pattern, a different man

You're not meeting them. You're choosing them.

Different man. Different city, different age, different job. He was nothing like the last one.

And here you are at month eight in the identical relationship, having the identical conversation, feeling the identical thing.

Here's the hard version of why.

You are not meeting these men. You're selecting them, out of a very large field, with extremely high accuracy.

Think about how many men you encounter. Hundreds, over a year. Most of them produce nothing. A handful produce something. And the ones who produce something share a profile, and the profile is specific, and your detection of it is fast and unconscious and remarkably reliable.

So the recurrence isn't luck. It's a search function running correctly, against criteria you didn't choose and can't see.

And here's what makes it so hard to catch. The selection happens before conscious evaluation. By the time you're assessing him on his merits, the choosing has already occurred. Your reasoning is downstream of a decision made in the first ten minutes, which is why your reasons are always good and the outcome is always the same.

Here's how to actually find your criteria, because "I have a type" isn't specific enough to be useful.

Take your last four, including the ones that didn't go anywhere but that you wanted. Write them in a row.

Now ignore everything about who they were. Jobs, looks, age, personality are usually noise. Write down instead, for each one: how available was he, emotionally, at the start?

That's the column that's usually identical.

Then: how certain was he about you in the first month? And: what did you have to do to get his attention?

Somewhere in those three columns is your pattern, and it will be the same answer four times.

What actually works.

Find the column that matches. Then name it precisely. Not "unavailable men" but the specific thing: men who are warm but vague, or men who are impressed by me, or men I have to win over, or men who need something.

Watch for the feeling, not the man. Your pattern announces itself as a feeling, usually in the first hour. Instant familiarity, or a specific kind of excitement, or the sense that he's slightly out of reach. When you notice that feeling, you've found the pattern, whatever he looks like.

And then run the experiment. Go on four dates with a man who produces none of that feeling. Not forever. Four. The point is to find out what you'd choose if the search function were off.

He was nothing like the last one. He had the one thing in common that actually matters, and it's the thing you've never looked at.

Your one move today.

Four men, three columns. Twenty minutes with a pen.

You'll see it immediately, and you won't be able to unsee it.
