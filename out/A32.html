What to say when he asks why you are distant

Four words. Not five.

Most women have been waiting for him to notice.

She's pulled back. She's been quieter. She's held her boundary. And now he's asked, and the question feels like a win, and in the next thirty seconds she gives all of it back.

Because she explains.

Here's the mechanism, and it's one of the most important things in my whole method.

Explanation is where power leaks.

When you explain yourself, three things happen at once. You hand him the material to argue with. You signal that you need his agreement. And you convert a fact into a proposal.

A woman who says "I've been busy" has stated a fact. There's nothing to do with it.

A woman who says "I've been busy, work has been really full on and then my sister needed me, and honestly I also felt like maybe I was being a bit much so I thought I'd give you some space" has handed him four separate things to respond to, revealed that she's been monitoring herself on his behalf, and asked, without asking, whether that was alright.

He will now reassure her, she will feel better, and the pull back will have achieved precisely nothing. Worse than nothing, because now he knows the distance was about him.

Here's the answer. It's four words.

"I've just been busy."

Then you stop. And then, this is the critical half, you move the conversation on. "Anyway, how was your week?"

Not defensive. Not cold. Warm, brief, and finished.

Here's why four words works where fifty doesn't.

It's unfalsifiable. He cannot argue with busy. It's not an accusation, so there's nothing to defend against, which means there's no argument available. And it's short enough that it clearly isn't a big deal to you, which is the actual message you're sending.

The content of the sentence is irrelevant. The length is the message.

What actually works.

Have the sentence ready before the moment comes. You will not improvise this well under pressure. Decide now.

Add nothing after it. The urge to soften it will be enormous. The softening is the leak.

Change the subject yourself. This is the move that makes it land. If you go quiet after the four words, it reads as loaded. If you cheerfully ask him something else, it reads as genuinely nothing.

And never, ever say "I just felt like you weren't making much effort." That's the honest version and it's the one that costs you everything. It tells him the distance was a message, and a message can be answered with words instead of behaviour.

The moment you explain your distance, it stops being distance and becomes a complaint. And complaints get managed, not answered.

Your one move today.

Say the sentence out loud. "I've just been busy." Then say "Anyway, how was your week?"

Practise it until it sounds bored. Bored is the target.

What do you normally say when he asks? Reply with your actual words, I'd like to see how long they are.
