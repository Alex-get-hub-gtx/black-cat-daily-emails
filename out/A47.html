What to do with an evening you used to give him

The void is the actual problem.

Most advice tells you to stop giving him so much of your time and then stops there.

Which leaves you with the actual problem, which is Thursday.

Because here's what happens. She pulls back, correctly. Thursday used to be his. Now Thursday is empty. And an empty Thursday is not neutral, it's a room with his name still on it, and by nine o'clock she's texting him.

The pull back failed not because the theory was wrong but because nobody handled the void.

Here's the mechanism.

You cannot remove a behaviour. You can only replace it.

Behaviour that fills a need does not disappear when you decide to stop it. It comes back, usually within a fortnight, usually at the exact time of day it lived. What works is substitution: something else that occupies the same slot and meets a similar need.

This is why "just stop texting him" fails and "I have pilates on Thursday now" works. Same outcome, completely different reliability.

So the question is never how to resist. It's what goes in the slot.

Here's how to do it properly.

First, identify the actual slot. Not "I spend too much time on him." Specifically: which evenings, and what time does it start? Most women have one or two recurring windows, and they know exactly when the pull begins. Usually between six and nine.

Second, fill it with something that has three properties. It's out of the house. It involves other people. And it's already booked, so it isn't a decision.

Out of the house matters because the house is where the behaviour lives. Other people matter because they make it hard to leave early. Pre-booked matters because at 7pm on a Thursday you will not choose to go.

Third, and this is the part people skip, make the first four weeks overkill. Book too much. The early weeks are when the pull is strongest and the structure needs to be heaviest. You can relax it later.

Here's what good substitutions look like.

A class with a start time. A friend you see on the same night every week. A sport. A shift at something. A standing dinner. A course with homework.

Here's what bad ones look like.

"I'll read more." "I'll focus on myself." "I'll do a face mask and watch something." None of those are substitutions. They're the same empty evening with better lighting, and he will be in your phone by nine.

What actually works.

Name the slot. Write down the day and the hour.

Book something into it that other people are expecting you at.

Overbook the first month, then ease off.

The pull back does not fail because you were weak. It fails because nobody told you to fill the hole you just made.

Your one move today.

Which evening was his? Name it.

Now book one thing into that evening for next week, with another person in it.
