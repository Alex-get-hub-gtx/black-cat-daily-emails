How to say no in four words

Every extra clause is a negotiation.

Most women cannot say no without a reason attached.

She says no, and then immediately explains why, and apologises, and offers an alternative, and by the end she's talked herself into a yes she didn't want.

Here's the mechanism and it's simple.

A reason is something that can be argued with. A no is not.

The moment you give a justification, you've converted your decision into a position in a debate. And he can now address the justification. "You're tired? We won't be late." "You're busy Thursday? What about Friday?"

He's not being manipulative. He's responding to the thing you handed him. You gave him an obstacle instead of an answer, and obstacles invite solutions.

So the rule is this: the no is the whole sentence.

"I can't, I've got something on." Four words plus a tiny bit of context that cannot be solved. Done.

Or even better, for anything that matters: "No, I don't want to."

That one is extraordinary and almost nobody can say it. It cannot be negotiated, it cannot be solved, and it tells him something enormous about you, which is that your preferences are sufficient grounds on their own.

Here's where it matters most.

Not the big things. The big things are easy, you're already saying no to those.

It's the small ones. The drink you don't fancy. The last minute plan. The favour. The thing at 10pm that you'd rather not. Those are where the pattern gets set, and the pattern is the whole relationship in miniature.

Every small yes you didn't mean teaches him that your no is a starting position.

What actually works.

Say no first, then stop. If you must add something, make it unsolvable. "I've got something on" cannot be fixed. "I'm quite tired" can.

Never apologise for a no. "Sorry, I can't" is twenty percent weaker than "I can't." The sorry says you've done something wrong.

Don't offer an alternative in the same breath. If you want to see him Friday, let that be a separate conversation on a separate day. Offering Friday while declining Thursday turns your no into a scheduling note.

And practise it on low stakes things. The waiter, the colleague, the friend who wants a favour. The muscle is the same muscle.

A no with a reason is an opening bid. A no on its own is a fact, and facts are the only things men actually plan around.

Your one move today.

Say no to one small thing today without a reason. Just "I can't" or "no, I'd rather not."

Then sit in the two seconds of discomfort afterwards. That discomfort is the entire price, and it's lower than you think.

What was the last yes you didn't mean? Reply and tell me, I ask this a lot and the answers are always so small and so telling.
