How to handle his family

They're not rating your warmth.

You want them to like you, so you go in warm. Helpful, agreeable, eager, complimentary. You offer to help in the kitchen four times.

And somehow you come away rated lower than the woman who sat down, was perfectly pleasant, and didn't try at all.

Here's the mechanism.

Families, like all small hierarchies, read composure before they read warmth.

Warmth from someone trying to be accepted registers as nervousness, and nervousness registers as low status. It's not that they dislike warmth. It's that effortful warmth tells them you need something from them, and needing something is the thing that positions you below.

Composure says the opposite. It says this woman is fine either way, which paradoxically makes her approval worth having.

And the person whose approval you're actually after, almost always, is his mother, and she is running the most sensitive version of this assessment in the room.

Here's what gets read as low.

Over-helping. Jumping up repeatedly, insisting on doing the washing up, apologising for sitting down. It looks like service rather than belonging.

Over-agreeing. Laughing at everything, having no opinions, deferring on every question. People cannot respect someone with no edges.

Looking at him for cues. This is the big one. Checking his face before you answer, letting him speak for you, deferring to him in front of them. It tells the whole room where you sit, and his mother will clock it within ten minutes.

Here's what reads as high, and it's much less effort.

Be warm and still. Sit down. Accept the drink. Say thank you once. Let them host you, because letting someone host you is a gift and over-helping takes it from them.

Have opinions, lightly held. Disagree about something unimportant, cheerfully. Families relax around people who are real.

Ask his mother about something only she knows. Not compliments about the house. A real question about her life, and then a second question. The ladder works here better than anywhere.

And be slightly less available to him in front of them. Don't orbit him. Talk to everyone else. A woman who is clearly adored and not clinging reads as a prize, and he will feel that in front of his family more intensely than anywhere else.

What actually works.

Help once, then stop. Offer once, accept the no, sit down.

Give her one real question and listen to the whole answer.

And don't look at him before you speak.

They are not deciding whether you're nice. They're deciding whether their son has done well, and that is a judgment about how you carry yourself, not about how helpful you are.

Your one move today.

Next time you're with them, help once and then sit down and stay down.

It'll feel rude. It reads as belonging.

How do you behave around his family? Reply and tell me, I think most of you over-help.

See you tomorrow!
