Leaving well

It preserves your standing and your options.

Most people leave badly, and leaving badly costs far more than the relationship did.

Here's what leaving well actually protects.

Your standing. You will see these people again. His friends, your mutual circle, the version of the story that circulates. A woman who left with composure is remembered as someone who knew what she wanted. A woman who left in a mess is remembered as the mess, however justified she was.

Your own account of yourself. This matters more than the social part. How you behave in the ending is what you carry, and it determines how long it takes to recover.

And your options. The way you end this determines whether you're free in six months or still entangled.

Here's how.

Decide alone, fully, before you say anything. The decision must be made before the conversation, not during it. If you go in undecided, you'll come out still in it.

Say it once, short, warm, no reasons. The four sentences. "I've thought about this a lot, and this isn't right for me. I'm not going to change my mind. I'm sorry, and I do wish you well."

No inventory. Do not list what he did wrong. It feels essential and it achieves nothing except handing him a list to rebut, and converting a decision into an argument.

No negotiation. He may offer to change. Some men will offer a lot in that moment. Take it as real and still leave, because a change produced by a departure needs months of evidence, and you cannot gather that evidence from inside.

Then the logistics, cleanly and fast. Practical things sorted quickly and without drama. Drawn out logistics are how people stay entangled for a year.

Then no contact. Not as punishment. Because of the extinction curve, and because every contact resets it.

And let him have the wrong version. He will tell a story in which you were cold or unreasonable or gave up. Correcting it costs you everything and changes nothing.

Here's the part about doing it well for him, which matters.

Don't leave slowly. The slow leave, where you're half out for eight months, is the cruellest version for both of you. Clean is kinder than gradual, even though gradual feels gentler at every individual step.

And don't leave in a way that makes him the villain to make it easier for yourself. You'll have to live with that account later.

What actually works.

Decide alone, say it once, no list, no negotiation.

Logistics fast, then zero contact.

And give yourself the six weeks rather than expecting to be fine.

How you leave is the last thing you'll have from this relationship, and it's the only part you get to keep.

Your one move today.

If you're close to this, write the four sentences now and put them somewhere.

Having the words is often what makes it possible.

What's stopping you, if something is? Reply, I read these ones closely.
