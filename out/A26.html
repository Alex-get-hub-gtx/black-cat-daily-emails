Your body knew before you did

That feeling was data.

Most women override the gut feeling because they cannot justify it.

Something is off. She can't point at anything. He's been perfectly nice and there's no evidence, so she decides she's being paranoid, and she carries on for another eight months.

Then it comes out, whatever it was, and the first thing she thinks is: I knew.

You did know. Here's how.

Your body processes patterns faster than your language does. The part of you that recognises a familiar shape in someone's behaviour runs well ahead of the part of you that can explain it.

So you get the signal before you get the sentence. The tight stomach, the drop in your chest, the sudden exhaustion after seeing him. That's not anxiety arriving from nowhere. That's your system having completed a calculation it cannot show you the working for.

The ability to read your own internal signals has a name, interoception, and it varies hugely between people. Some women are very good at it and have been talked out of trusting it their entire lives.

Here's the catch, and I have to be honest about it.

Your gut is not always right. An anxious nervous system produces false positives constantly. If you've been hurt badly, your system will flag safe men as dangerous, and if you follow every signal you'll leave good relationships.

So the skill isn't blind trust. The skill is telling the two apart, and there's a clean way to do it.

A true signal is specific and it's about him. It shows up in response to something he did, it has a shape, and it goes away when the behaviour changes. "Something was off about how he answered that" is a true signal.

A false positive is general and it's about you. It shows up when nothing has happened, often when things are going well. It attaches to the relationship rather than to a behaviour. "I feel like it's going to be taken away" is a false positive, and it's usually your history talking.

One points at a thing he did. The other points at a fear you brought.

What actually works.

Write it down when it happens. One line, with the date, and what he'd just said or done. Don't interpret it. Just log it. After six weeks you'll have a document, and the pattern in it is far more persuasive than any single feeling.

Ask the shape question. Is this about something specific he did, or is this the background hum? Two completely different answers.

And never argue yourself out of a signal because you lack evidence. Lack of evidence is not evidence. Hold it as open, keep logging, and let him either resolve it or confirm it.

You were not being paranoid. You were running a calculation faster than you could narrate it, and then you talked yourself out of the answer.

Your one move today.

Start the log. One note on your phone, called nothing. Next time your stomach drops, one line and the date.

In six weeks you'll have something nobody can talk you out of.

Have you ever known before you knew? Reply and tell me the story, these are the ones I find hardest to forget.
