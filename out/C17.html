How to be a wife and still be the prize

The dynamic collapses at the ceremony.

Something happens at the wedding, and it's not romantic.

The uncertainty ends. Permanently, formally, publicly. And every mechanism that made you compelling ran on some amount of uncertainty.

Here's what actually collapses.

Loss aversion stops operating. Until now, there was always a version of the future where you weren't there. That possibility was doing enormous work, invisibly, in how he treated you. Marriage removes it, by design, because removing it is the entire point.

The pursuit ends, because it's been concluded.

And your availability becomes total, because you now live in the same house and share the same name and the same calendar.

So the question is not how to keep him uncertain. You've just spent a great deal to remove his uncertainty and that was correct.

The question is what replaces the mechanism.

Here's the answer, and it's the same one from a different angle.

Certainty about the relationship. Uncertainty about who you're becoming.

He should have total security that you love him and you're not going anywhere. That's the floor and it should never wobble.

What he should not have is a complete and final picture of who you are. Because if the woman he married at thirty two is identical at forty two, the pursuit genuinely is over, not through any failure of his, but because there's nothing left to approach.

Here's the practical version.

Keep becoming someone. A new skill, a career change, a subject, a body, a project. A woman in motion is never finished, and a man cannot be bored of someone who is still being written.

Keep your name on things and your own money, for all the reasons in the last email, and because a woman with options behaves differently and he can feel it.

Keep your own friends and your own evenings. Marriage tends to merge social worlds completely, and the merge is where separateness goes.

And refuse the role, quietly. There's a wife-shaped set of expectations that arrives with the ring, about who does the admin, who remembers the birthdays, who manages the emotional life of the household. Those roles are assumed by default and they reduce a woman to a function. The time to decline them is the first six months, before they set.

What actually works.

Decide what you're not taking on, early, and simply don't take it on.

Keep one thing you're building that is entirely yours.

And keep the Thursday.

You did not stop being the prize when you got married. You stopped when you became a function, and the function arrived gradually while everyone was being very happy for you.
