He changed and you did not trust it

Your system is still on the old model.

He's doing it. The thing you asked for, the thing you waited for. He's consistent now.

And you're watching him like a hawk.

Every good week feels like the quiet before something. You're looking for the crack. And part of you is almost waiting for it to fail so you can stop holding your breath.

Here's what's happening and it's not sabotage, it's physiology.

Your nervous system is still running the threat model it built during the bad months, and it does not update just because the facts changed.

Here's why. Threat learning is fast and it's sticky by design. One bad experience is enough to install it. Safety learning is slow, and it requires repeated evidence over time, and crucially it never fully overwrites the original. It builds a second, competing model alongside it.

Which means you can know he's changed and still feel like he hasn't. Both are running. The knowing is new and quiet. The feeling is old and loud.

And here's the trap. The hypervigilance feels like wisdom. It feels like you're being sensible, not getting fooled twice, keeping your eyes open. It presents itself as discernment.

It isn't discernment. Discernment looks at evidence. Hypervigilance looks for confirmation of something it already believes, and it will find it, because anyone can be made to look suspicious if you watch them closely enough for long enough.

Here's how this breaks a good thing.

He's fine, and she's testing. Small tests, constant ones. Will he notice, will he follow through, will he choose her this time.

And he keeps passing, and nothing changes, because the tests aren't actually about him. So she escalates, because the reassurance isn't landing, because reassurance never lands in a system that's looking for threat.

Eventually he gets tired. Not of her. Of being on trial for something he didn't do.

What actually works.

Count the evidence deliberately, because your system won't. Write down what he actually did each week. Facts only. After two months you'll have a document that argues with the feeling, and the document is more reliable than you are on this.

Name it when it happens. "This is the old model." Saying it out loud separates the feeling from the situation, and that separation is most of the work.

Give it longer than feels reasonable. Safety learning is slow. Six months of consistency is roughly what it takes, and expecting it at six weeks is how women conclude it isn't working.

And stop running tests. Every test you run, you're asking a question you've already got the answer to, and the asking is what's eroding it.

He is not making you feel unsafe. The last one is, and he is not in the room, and your body has not been told.

Your one move today.

Start the evidence log. One line a week. What he actually did.

Two months from now that page will be the thing that lets you relax.
