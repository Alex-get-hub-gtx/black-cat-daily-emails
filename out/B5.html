What to do when he calls you cold

Three words.

At some point he'll say it.

"You've been really distant lately." Or "you've gone cold on me." Or the sharper one, "I don't know what I did."

And in that moment everything you've built either holds or collapses, and it usually collapses, because the accusation does exactly what it's designed to do.

Here's what's actually happening.

It's a test, and it's often not even a conscious one.

He's noticed the change. The change is uncomfortable. And the fastest route back to comfort is to make the change your problem rather than his. If you can be persuaded that you're being cold, you'll correct it, and everything returns to how it was.

He may not be scheming. Most men aren't. But the function of the sentence is the same either way: it transfers responsibility for the distance from his behaviour to your character.

And the reason it works so well on good women is that you don't want to be cold. Coldness is the thing you've been told your whole life not to be. So the accusation lands on something tender and you move to disprove it immediately.

Which means you give everything back inside one conversation.

Here's the answer.

"I'm not, though."

Said warmly. Lightly. Almost amused. And then you move on, exactly like you did with "I've just been busy."

Three words. Not defensive, not an apology, not an explanation, and crucially not a denial that sounds like a defence. Just a mild, friendly correction of a factual error.

Here's why it works.

It doesn't engage the frame. Any longer answer accepts the premise that your warmth is on trial and now needs defending. "I'm not, though" declines the whole proceeding.

It's warm, so it doesn't confirm the accusation. This is the part that matters. If you go quiet or defensive, you've proved his point. If you're light and affectionate while declining to change anything, you've disproved it without conceding an inch.

And it ends the conversation. There's nowhere for him to go. He can't argue with it and he can't escalate without looking like he's making a thing of it.

What actually works.

Say it warmly and then change the subject yourself. Same move as before. The subject change is what signals this isn't a big deal to you.

Do not list your reasons. Not work, not your sister, not anything. The moment you supply a cause, he can address the cause.

And don't correct your behaviour afterwards to prove it. That's the real test, and it's run in the following week, not in that conversation.

He is not asking whether you've gone cold. He is asking whether saying so is enough to get the old arrangement back.

Your one move today.

Say it out loud. "I'm not, though." Light, warm, slightly amused.

Have it ready, because you will not come up with it in the moment.
