Choosing, instead of being chosen

The last upgrade.

Here's the shift that changes everything upstream of it.

You've spent your life being assessed. Being the one who is looked at, evaluated, picked or not picked. Everything you've ever been taught about being a woman in this context has been about improving your position in someone else's decision.

And at some point it has to invert, or none of the rest of it ever becomes real.

Here's what choosing actually requires, and it's more than people think.

It requires criteria. Written, specific, decided in advance, by you, when nobody is in front of you.

It requires the ability to see rather than hope. That's a skill, and it's largely the skill of noticing what he does and refusing to average it with what he says.

It requires a deadline, privately held.

And it requires being able to say no to something good enough, which is the hardest of the four by an enormous margin.

Here's what makes that last one possible, and there's only one thing.

A life you'd be content to keep.

Not content as in resigned. Content as in genuinely good. Full week, own money, real friends, something you're building, a Tuesday you like. If that exists, you can decline a man who is fine, and declining a man who is fine is the single act that determines the rest of your life.

If it doesn't exist, you cannot decline anything, and so you are not choosing. You're accepting with extra steps.

Which is why everything in all of these emails has pointed at the same place, over and over. It was never about men. It was about building the position from which a woman can actually say no.

Here's what it looks like from the outside, and it's the thing people misread as confidence.

A woman who is choosing is warm, interested, generous, and entirely unhurried. She's delighted by a good man. She's unmoved by a mediocre one. She isn't testing, she isn't auditioning, and there's no performance in any direction.

That posture cannot be acted. It rests on the life. That's why it works and why nobody can fake it for more than a few weeks.

What actually works.

Write the criteria. Ten of them, specific, including the unflattering ones. Most women have never done this.

Practise declining. Say no to one good-enough thing a month, in any area of life. It's the same muscle.

And keep building the life you'd keep, especially once you have the relationship. That's when it starts getting dismantled, and that's when it matters most.

Being chosen was never the achievement. Being a woman who could have said no, and didn't, is the whole thing.

Your one move today.

Write the ten. Properly, not in your head.

Then look at whether your current situation would have made the list.
