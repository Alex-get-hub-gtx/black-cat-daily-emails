The one conversation worth having

Four sentences, once.

I've just spent an email telling you not to have the conversation.

Here's the exception, and how to do it so it works.

There is one conversation worth having, and it's the one where you need a fact that cannot be observed.

Does he want children. Is he seeing other people. Does he intend to stay in this city. Is he ever going to get divorced. These are not dynamics, they're facts, and no amount of behavioural change will reveal them in a useful timeframe.

So you ask. Once. And here's how.

Four sentences. No more.

One: the context, in a single line, with no history attached. "I want to ask you something straight."

Two: the actual question, as a closed question. Not "how do you feel about children" but "do you want children." Open questions get essays. Closed questions get answers.

Three: the silence. This is a sentence because it's that important. You ask, and then you stop talking, and you do not fill the gap no matter how long it goes on. The gap is where the real answer lives. Most women rescue the man from the silence inside four seconds and never find out what he was going to say.

Four: the acknowledgement. Whatever he says, "okay, thank you for being straight with me." And then you leave it. You do not respond to it, negotiate it, or react to it in that conversation.

That's it. Ninety seconds.

Here's what makes this work and what breaks it.

It works because it's unambiguous and undramatic and it doesn't invite discussion. He can't manage his way out of a closed question asked calmly.

It breaks when you explain why you're asking. The moment you say "because I've been thinking about where this is going and I don't want to waste my time", you've converted a question into a complaint, and he'll respond to the complaint.

It also breaks when you react in the room. If he says something you don't want to hear and you argue with it, he'll revise it to something more comfortable, and now you have a managed answer instead of a true one. Take the answer away and decide about it later, alone.

And have it once. Once. Asking the same question in three months tells him the first answer wasn't binding.

What actually works.

Write the four sentences before you have it. You will not do this well improvising.

Ask a closed question, then be quiet.

Thank him, end it, and decide afterwards.

And believe the first answer. The first answer is almost always the true one. Everything after it is adjustment.

The conversation only works when it is short, closed, and asked by a woman who is genuinely prepared to hear either answer.

Your one move today.

If there's a fact you need, write your four sentences now.

Then read sentence three again, because that's the one that decides the outcome.
