How to be alone without being lonely

This is the root skill.

Most women are not choosing men. They're avoiding an evening.

She won't admit it in those words. But the reason she goes back, the reason she answers at 11pm, the reason she accepts the arrangement, is that the alternative is a Tuesday on her own and she cannot do a Tuesday on her own.

Here's why this is the root of everything.

Every single thing I teach is downstream of your tolerance for your own company.

You cannot hold a boundary if being alone is unbearable, because the boundary might result in being alone. You cannot walk away. You cannot wait. You cannot let a gap exist. Your capacity to do any of it is capped by your capacity to be by yourself on a Tuesday.

So this is not a nice spiritual extra. It's the load bearing wall.

And here's the distinction that makes it workable.

Loneliness is not the same as being alone. Loneliness is being alone with no relationship to yourself. The problem is not the empty room. It's that there's nobody in it, including you.

Which means the skill is not learning to endure solitude. It's building something to be alone with.

Here's what it looks like when it's missing.

Six o'clock arrives and the evening is empty, and there's a specific dread. So she scrolls. She texts someone. She checks his profile. She fills it with something, anything, because the unstructured version is intolerable.

And every one of those fills is a small payment she makes to avoid the thing. And the thing never gets smaller, because avoidance is what keeps it big.

What actually works, and this is practical, not philosophical.

Give the evening a shape. Not a busy schedule. A shape. Food you actually cooked, a bath, a book, a film you chose. The dread comes from formlessness, not from solitude, and a shape dissolves most of it.

Do one thing alone in public every week. Coffee, lunch, a gallery, a film. On your own, no phone propped up as a shield. This is the specific muscle and it's the one most women have never trained. The first three times are uncomfortable and then something switches.

Have one thing you only do alone. A route you walk, a bath ritual, a cafe, a Sunday morning. Something you'd actually be slightly annoyed to have company for. That's the marker that it's working.

And notice the 6pm feeling without acting on it. Just once. Let it be there for twenty minutes without filling it. It passes. It always passes, and finding that out first-hand is worth more than anything I can tell you.

You will keep going back to men you don't want until an ordinary Tuesday on your own stops being something you'd pay to avoid.

Your one move today.

Book one thing this week that you'll do alone, in public. Lunch is the easiest. No phone on the table.

That's the whole exercise.

What's your 6pm feeling? Reply and describe it, I think a lot of you have never put it into words.
