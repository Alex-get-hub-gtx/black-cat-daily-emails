Voice, pace, and the authority of slowness

It's not what you say.

Everything you've built can be undone by how you sound, and almost nobody works on this.

Here's what actually carries authority, and none of it is content.

Pace. Slow reads as high status, universally. Fast reads as anxious or as someone who expects to be interrupted. Most women speak faster than they need to because somewhere they learned they had limited time to get it out.

Pitch floor. Not speaking deeply, which sounds affected. Letting your sentences land at the bottom of your range rather than rising. A statement that ends upward is a question, and a woman whose statements all sound like questions is asking permission continuously.

Pause length. This is the big one. The ability to leave two full seconds of silence before answering, or in the middle of a sentence, is the single most powerful vocal behaviour there is. It signals that you're not worried about losing the floor.

Most people cannot do it. The discomfort of the pause is enormous and almost everyone fills it, and filling it is where authority leaks.

Volume. Lower than you think. People lean in for someone quiet. Loud is what you do when you're not sure you're being listened to.

And the qualifiers. "Just", "sorry", "I think maybe", "does that make sense", "I could be wrong but". Each one is a small withdrawal of your own claim. Removing them is the fastest single improvement available and it takes about a fortnight of noticing.

Here's why this matters more at your level than at any other.

You've done the internal work. You have the life, the pillars, the standards. And if you still sound like someone asking permission, none of it transmits, because people respond to the delivery long before they process the content.

This is the layer where a lot of very capable women stay invisible.

What actually works.

Halve your pace for one conversation a day. Just one. It feels absurd and it's unmistakably different to everyone listening.

Let sentences fall at the end. Practise it on something boring, like ordering coffee.

Pause for two seconds before you answer anything that matters. Count it. The discomfort is the training.

And cut one qualifier. Pick "just" or "sorry" and remove it for a month. You'll be astonished how often it was there.

You can have everything in order and still be spoken over, because the room is responding to your pace and not your position.

Your one move today.

Pick one qualifier you use and count how many times you say it today.

Just counting it starts removing it.

Which one is yours? Reply, mine was "sorry" and it took a year.
