Your anxiety is about the situation

Not about you.

Most women decide their anxiety is a character flaw.

I'm too much. I'm insecure. I need to work on myself before I can have a good relationship. She reads about anxious attachment and takes it on as an identity and makes it her project.

Here's what I'd want you to consider instead.

Anxiety in a relationship is usually an accurate reading of an inconsistent environment.

Your system is built to track whether connection is reliable. That's not a bug, it's close to its main job. When the signals from someone are consistent, it settles. When they're unpredictable, warm then distant, available then gone, it escalates. It has to. An inconsistent attachment is genuinely a risk, and your system is correctly treating it as one.

So the anxiety is not malfunctioning. It's reporting.

And here's where the harm happens. She takes a correct reading of a bad situation and relabels it as a flaw in herself. Now she's not evaluating him. She's managing her symptoms. She's doing breathwork so she can tolerate something that should not be tolerated.

That's the part I want to stop.

Let me be careful and fair about this, because both things are true.

There is such a thing as anxiety you brought with you. If you're anxious with a man whose behaviour is genuinely consistent, who turns up, who is clear, who does what he says, then yes, that's yours, and that's real work, and it's worth doing.

But if you're anxious with a man who is hot and cold, who is vague about the future, who goes quiet for days, who is lovely in private and distant in public? That's not your anxious attachment. That's accurate perception.

Here's the test, and it's one question.

Is his behaviour predictable?

If yes, and you're still anxious, the work is internal. If no, then your nervous system is not the problem that needs solving.

What actually works.

Run the test honestly before you take the blame. Write down the last four weeks. Would someone who didn't know you call that behaviour consistent?

Stop using regulation to tolerate the intolerable. The breathing, the journalling, the self-soothing, all of it is for living well. It is not for making an unreliable man survivable.

And change your question. Not "why am I like this". Ask "what is my body responding to". It's a completely different investigation and it usually has an answer.

A smoke alarm going off is not a faulty smoke alarm. Check for the fire before you take the battery out.

Your one move today.

Write down his last four weeks as a list of facts. No feelings, no interpretations. Just what happened and when.

Then read it as though it were a friend's. You'll know instantly whose anxiety this is.

Have you been told you're too anxious by a man who was being inconsistent? Reply, I think a lot of you have.
