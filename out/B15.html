Why he commits to the woman who was leaving

Not a threat. A departure.

You've watched it happen. Maybe to a friend, maybe to you from the wrong side.

Three years of nothing. She finally decides she's done, genuinely done, and starts to go. And within weeks he's proposing.

And everyone says ultimatums work after all. They don't. Something else happened and the difference matters enormously.

Here's the mechanism.

An ultimatum is a statement about the future that invites negotiation. A departure is a change in the present that requires a decision.

When she says "if you don't commit by Christmas I'm leaving", he hears a deadline he can manage. There's time. There's a conversation to be had. There are things he can say that will move the date. And underneath all of it, the fact that she announced it tells him she'd rather stay, which is the only information he needs.

When she actually starts leaving, nothing is being negotiated. She's looking at flats. She's stopped making plans past next month. She's different in a way he can feel and cannot argue with.

Now he's not choosing between committing and continuing. He's choosing between committing and losing her. Those are completely different decisions and only the second one produces action.

Here's the piece underneath it, and it's the same thing I've said about loss aversion.

People do not act to gain. They act to avoid loss. A man weighing up whether to commit is evaluating a gain, and gains are easy to defer indefinitely. A man watching a woman leave is facing a loss, and losses are urgent.

Nothing about her value changed. The frame changed from acquisition to loss, and that's the whole thing.

Now, the honest part, because this is where it gets misused.

You cannot fake this. A woman who starts leaving in order to produce a proposal is running an ultimatum with extra steps, and he will feel it, because she's watching him while she does it.

The reason it works when it works is that she's actually gone. The decision was made for her own reasons and his response is not the point. That's precisely what makes it real.

Which means the only safe way to use this is not to use it. Decide what you're doing based on your own life, act on it, and let his response be his response.

And be prepared for the other outcome. Sometimes she leaves and he lets her, and that is also an answer, delivered much faster than it otherwise would have been.

What actually works.

Make the decision for your reasons, not as a move. The moment it's a move, it stops working.

Don't announce it. The announcement is what converts it back into a negotiation.

And if he does come forward, slow down. A proposal produced by panic needs testing. Give it six months of changed behaviour before you treat it as real, because the panic wears off and what's underneath is what you'll be living with.

He did not commit because she threatened to go. He committed because she went, and the difference is that one of those was never about him.

Your one move today.

Ask yourself what you'd do if his response genuinely didn't matter.

That answer is the one worth acting on, and it's the only one that works anyway.
