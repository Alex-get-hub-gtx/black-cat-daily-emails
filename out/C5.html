Self sabotage at the moment of getting it

The system defends the familiar.

It's going well. Properly well. And you do something.

You pick a fight about nothing. You go cold for no reason. You bring up something from four months ago. You say the thing you know will hurt.

And afterwards you genuinely cannot explain it, which is the part that frightens you most.

Here's the mechanism, and it's not about deserving.

Any system defends its normal state, and a good relationship is not your normal state.

Think about what's actually happening when things go well after years of them not going well. Every signal is unfamiliar. Nothing to manage, nothing to decode, nobody to win over. A kind of silence where the vigilance used to be.

And that silence is not experienced as peace. It's experienced as wrongness. The body does not have a category for "good and safe", it only has "familiar" and "unfamiliar", and unfamiliar triggers the same alarm whether the thing is bad or wonderful.

So the sabotage is not self-destruction. It's a return to a known state. Creating a problem restores the conditions you know how to operate in. It's awful, and it's also, in its own way, a kind of relief, which is why you keep doing it.

Here's the shape it takes, and knowing the shape is most of the defence.

It arrives after a high point, usually within three days. Something good happens, there's a lovely weekend, he says something real. And then a window opens where everything he does is irritating and a case assembles itself in your mind.

It feels completely justified at the time. That's the critical part. It never feels like sabotage. It feels like finally seeing clearly.

And it's often triggered by his vulnerability specifically. The moment he's fully open is often the moment the hostility arrives, because that's the moment of maximum exposure.

What actually works.

Impose a delay. Seventy two hours between feeling it and acting on it. No ending things, no big conversations, no cold snaps inside that window. This single rule prevents most of the damage.

Tell him in advance, in general terms, once, while things are good. Not as a warning, as information. "Sometimes when things go well I get strange for a few days, it passes, it isn't about you." A man who's been told this can hold steady through it. A man who hasn't will reasonably conclude you've gone off him.

Notice the trigger. Track what happened in the forty eight hours before. It's almost always a moment of closeness.

And do not make permanent decisions during the window. Nothing structural, nothing said that can't be unsaid.

You are not ruining it because you don't want it. You're restoring a state your body recognises, and the only thing that changes that is going through the window without acting on it, enough times that good stops being unfamiliar.

Your one move today.

Write down the last time you did it, and what happened in the two days before.

That's your trigger, and it will be the same one next time.
