He is not confused. He is comfortable

Confusion is the polite word.

Most women spend months trying to solve his confusion.

She's patient. She gives him space to work it out. She reads about avoidant attachment. She tries to understand what's going on inside him, because surely a man who behaves this warmly must be wrestling with something.

He isn't wrestling with anything. He's comfortable.

Here's the mechanism.

People do not change an arrangement that's working for them. Not out of malice. It's just that there's no mechanism to make them. Change requires either a better option or a cost, and if neither exists, the current state continues indefinitely.

Confusion is what a man says when the current arrangement is already optimal for him.

Think about what "I don't know what I want" actually achieves for him. It keeps you. It commits him to nothing. It makes him sympathetic rather than selfish. And it transfers the entire problem to you, because now you're the one managing it, researching it, being patient about it.

It's not a lie, exactly. He probably does feel a bit confused, in the way anyone feels a bit confused about whether to leave a comfortable room.

Here's the thing to watch.

Confusion in men is extremely selective. He's not confused about his job. He's not confused about whether he's going to that festival in June. He's not confused about his gym schedule or which car he wants or who he's spending Christmas with.

He's confused about one single thing, and it's the one thing where clarity would cost him something.

A man who is genuinely confused about a woman behaves erratically. He's hot, then cold, then he disappears for a week, then he's back, because real uncertainty is uncomfortable and it shows.

A man who is comfortable behaves with total consistency. Same warmth, same access, same vagueness, for two years. There's nothing uncertain about that pattern at all. It's the most stable thing in his life.

What actually works.

Stop solving it. Every hour you spend understanding his confusion is an hour you spend making the arrangement more comfortable. You are the reason it's sustainable.

Introduce the only two things that produce change: a cost, or a better option. And the better option doesn't have to be another man. A full life is a better option. He should be able to feel that your week does not require him.

And stop asking. Each conversation about where this is going is a reminder that the vagueness is working, because you keep accepting the answer.

He is not lost. He is parked. And he will stay parked until the parking stops being free.

Your one move today.

Count the months. Just count them. Then ask yourself one question: in all that time, has the arrangement ever changed because of something you said?

If the answer is no, you already know that talking isn't the lever.

How long have you been in a comfortable one? Reply with the number. Saying it plainly does something.

See you tomorrow!
