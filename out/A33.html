How to end a conversation first

The last thirty seconds is what he keeps.

Most women stay until the conversation dies.

She's enjoying it, he's enjoying it, so she keeps going. Then it slows, the replies get shorter, and eventually one of them stops answering and it just ends, flatly, with nobody deciding.

Here's why that costs you more than you think.

What people remember about an experience is disproportionately the end of it.

Your memory doesn't average an experience. It keeps the peak and it keeps the end, and the end carries enormous weight. This is well established and it's why the last five minutes of a holiday can colour the whole fortnight.

So the question is not how good the conversation was. The question is what state he was in when it finished.

If you ride it until it dies, the last thing he has of you is a fizzle. Slightly flat, slightly less interested, nothing happening. That's what gets filed.

If you leave at the peak, the last thing he has of you is wanting more. And he'll carry that for hours, and it'll be the thing that makes him pick his phone up tomorrow.

Same conversation. Completely different residue.

Here's the move, precisely.

You're mid-flow. It's good. You're laughing. That's the moment. "Right, I'm going. This was lovely." And go.

Not "I should probably go." Not "sorry, I've got to head off, I'd love to carry on though." Those are apologies, and an apology tells him you didn't want to leave, which undoes it.

Just: this was lovely, I'm off. Warm and final.

Same thing in person, and in person it's even stronger. You're at the table, it's going beautifully, and you leave. First. While he's still enjoying it.

I know how counterintuitive this feels. You've got something good and the instinct is to stay in it. But staying in it is how it becomes ordinary, and leaving it is how it becomes a thing he thinks about in the car.

What actually works.

Decide your exit before you start. On a date, know roughly when you're leaving, and leave then even if it's going brilliantly. Especially if it's going brilliantly.

Leave on a high note, not a low one. The test: if you're wondering whether to go, you're already slightly past the moment. Go now.

And never explain your exit. "I'm off" needs no reason. A reason invites negotiation.

The woman who leaves first is the one he's still thinking about. The woman who stays to the end is the one he had a nice evening with.

Your one move today.

Next conversation, with anyone, end it first and end it warm. Practise on a friend if you like.

You'll feel the pull to stay. That pull is the exact thing you're training against.

Have you ever been the one to leave first? How did it feel? Reply, I think most of you have never tried it.
