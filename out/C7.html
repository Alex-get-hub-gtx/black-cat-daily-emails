What becoming magnetic costs

Nobody mentions the price.

Everybody sells you the upgrade. Nobody tells you the bill.

So let me, because you're far enough in that you've probably started receiving it and assumed something was going wrong.

Here's what changes when you change.

Some friendships end. Specifically the ones that ran on shared complaint. If a friendship was built on both of you being hard done by, and you stop being hard done by, there's nothing holding it together. It usually doesn't end in a row. It just goes quiet, and you'll feel disloyal, and there's no way around it.

Some people will tell you you've changed, and they won't mean it kindly. Particularly the people who were comfortable with the old arrangement. A woman who stops over-giving becomes inconvenient to everyone who was receiving, and they rarely say that directly. They say you've become cold, or hard, or that you think you're better than everyone.

Your family may be the hardest. Family systems have roles, and if you were the accommodating one, your becoming less accommodating is a structural change to a system that isn't asking for one.

And the loneliest part: you'll be able to see the thing you used to do, in other women, clearly, and you won't be able to say anything about it. Watching a friend do what you used to do and knowing exactly how it ends is a specific kind of helpless.

Here's the part that's harder than the social cost.

You lose a story about yourself, and that story was doing work. Being the one who tries hardest, who loves most, who gives everything, is painful and it's also an identity, and it comes with a certain dignity.

Letting it go means accepting that the suffering wasn't noble. It was just expensive. And a lot of women would genuinely rather be tragic than ordinary, and I don't say that as a criticism, I say it because the pull is real and it's worth naming.

What actually works.

Expect the friendship attrition and don't fight it. Let the ones that were built on complaint go quietly. Make one new friendship a year with someone who is where you want to be.

Hear "you've changed" as confirmation. It is, almost always, being said by someone who preferred the old terms.

And grieve the old identity properly rather than pretending you don't miss it. There was something in it. Say what it was, to yourself, once, and then let it go.

Every woman who has done this has paid in the same currency. Nobody warns you, so you assume you've done something wrong, and you haven't.

Your one move today.

Name one relationship that's changed since you started this. Just name it.

You don't have to do anything about it. Noticing it isn't your fault is the move.

What has it cost you so far? Reply and tell me. I'd like to know what people actually find.
