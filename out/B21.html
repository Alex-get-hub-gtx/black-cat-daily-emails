How to handle his phone

Looking costs you the position.

You want to look. Maybe you have looked.

And I'm not going to tell you that you're being paranoid, because sometimes you're not. But I am going to tell you what looking costs, because nobody does the maths before they do it.

Here's the mechanism.

Surveillance transfers power, every single time, regardless of what you find.

Think through the three possible outcomes.

You find nothing. Now you have temporary relief and a habit. The relief lasts about four days, because nothing found is not the same as nothing there, and the only way to maintain the relief is to look again. You have installed a compulsion and solved nothing.

You find something ambiguous. A message that could mean anything. Now you're in the worst position available: you know something, you can't ask about it without admitting how you know, and you're going to spend three weeks interpreting six words.

You find something real. And here, finally, something useful has happened, but notice the shape of it. You now have information you cannot use cleanly, obtained in a way that hands him a counter-accusation, and the conversation becomes about your looking rather than about his doing.

In all three outcomes, you've moved from the position of a woman being pursued to the position of a woman investigating. Those are not compatible. You cannot be the prize and the detective in the same relationship.

Here's the thing underneath it.

The urge to look is almost never about the phone. It's about an unresolved feeling you can't settle, and you've located a device that promises to settle it.

It won't. Certainty obtained by surveillance has a half-life of days, because the method that produced it can always be run again, and a mind that knows it can check will always need to check.

So the question is not what's on his phone. It's what you're going to do about the feeling.

And you already know the answer to that one, because it's the same answer as everything else: either his behaviour is consistent, in which case the feeling is yours and it needs regulating, or his behaviour is inconsistent, in which case you don't need the phone, you already have the data.

What actually works.

Go to the behaviour, not the device. Write down the last four weeks as facts. The answer is usually sitting there in plain sight.

If you have a real question, ask it once, closed, calmly. "Are you seeing anyone else." Then watch the following month.

And if you've been looking, stop, and don't confess it. Confessing converts your concern into his grievance.

Whatever is on his phone, the act of looking has already cost you more than the information is worth.

Your one move today.

Write the four weeks of facts instead. Just that.

You'll find you already know what you were going to go looking for.

Have you looked? Reply, honestly, nobody sees this but me.

See you tomorrow!
