Your style as a signal

It's information, not decoration.

How you dress is not about being attractive. At this level it's about what you're telling a room before you speak.

Here's the frame.

Clothing is information about status, intent, and self-regard, and it's read instantly and unconsciously by everybody.

Four things get read, and almost nobody manages them deliberately.

Fit. The single highest-signal variable, higher than price by a long way. Well-fitted inexpensive clothing reads higher than expensive clothing that doesn't fit, every time, because fit signals that you paid attention and that you consider yourself worth the effort.

Restraint. How much is happening. More elements read as trying harder, and trying harder reads as lower status. The most expensive-looking people in any room are almost always wearing the fewest things.

Condition. Shoes, nails, hems, the state of your coat. People read condition as a proxy for how you treat yourself, and they're mostly right.

And congruence. Whether you look like someone who belongs where you are. Overdressing reads as effort, underdressing as indifference, and both are noticed.

Here's the thing that's specific to this.

There's a difference between dressing to be wanted and dressing to be reckoned with, and most women have only ever been taught the first.

Dressing to be wanted optimises for male attention: more skin, more emphasis, more signal. It works, and what it attracts is attention, which, as we've established, is cheap.

Dressing to be reckoned with optimises for status: fit, restraint, quality, congruence. It attracts less attention and much better attention, and it changes how you're treated by everyone in the room, including the women, which is where your status actually gets set.

You know which rooms want which. Most women over-index on the first long after it's stopped serving them.

And the practical layer that matters more than any of this: a small wardrobe of things that fit perfectly and that you like, worn repeatedly, beats a large wardrobe of nearly-right things. Every well-dressed person you know has a uniform.

What actually works.

Get three things altered this month. Alterations are cheap and the effect is immediate and larger than buying anything new.

Remove one element before you leave. The old rule and it's still the best one.

Fix condition first. Shoes resoled, hems repaired, the coat cleaned. It's the cheapest upgrade available.

And build a uniform rather than a collection.

You are not dressing to be looked at. You're dressing to establish what kind of woman has just walked in, and that gets decided before anyone has spoken to you.

Your one move today.

Pick three things you own that nearly fit, and book the alterations.

That's the highest return thing you'll do this month.
