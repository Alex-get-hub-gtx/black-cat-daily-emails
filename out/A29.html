How to break an attachment

Time alone does not do it.

Most women are told that time heals it. So she waits. And a year later she's still checking his profile on a Sunday and she decides she must be broken.

Time does not break an attachment. Time plus zero contact breaks an attachment, and the zero is the whole thing.

Here's the mechanism.

When a conditioned behaviour stops being rewarded, it fades. That's extinction, and it has a predictable shape.

The shape matters. It is not a smooth decline. It goes like this.

Days one and two are usually oddly calm. You feel resolved. Strong, even. This fools everybody.

Days three to five are the worst. There's a spike, and it's a real one. The urge gets stronger than it was before you stopped, because the system is escalating the behaviour that used to work. In the literature this is the burst you get right before a behaviour fades, and it is the single most common point at which women break and message him.

Week two, it drops noticeably. Not gone. Noticeably lower.

Weeks three to six, it keeps falling, with random spikes from triggers. A song, a street, a Thursday.

And then here's the critical part. One contact resets the whole thing to day one.

Not halfway. To day one. Because the behaviour just got rewarded after a long gap, which is the strongest possible reinforcement there is. That's why the woman who goes four weeks and then has one lovely conversation is suddenly back to where she started and feels insane about it.

She's not insane. She just paid for four weeks and spent it in one evening.

What actually works.

Zero means zero. Not his stories, not his profile, not his friend's profile, not asking about him. Every look is a small reward and every small reward extends the curve.

Block rather than resist. Not out of anger. Out of arithmetic. You are not strong enough to be tested forty times a day, and nobody is, and relying on willpower here is like deciding not to be hungry.

Plan for day four specifically. Know it's coming. Put something in the diary for that night. The spike is survivable when you know it's a spike and not a sign.

And expect the random triggers for months. They're not evidence you made a mistake. They're the last bits of the pattern discharging.

An attachment does not fade with time. It fades with time in which nothing rewards it, and one message buys back everything you paid.

Your one move today.

If you're in it, block today, and put something in your calendar for four days from now.

Day four is where this is won or lost, and you can decide about it in advance.
