He is all in and you feel nothing

The pursuit was the reward.

He's chosen you. Fully, visibly, without ambivalence. He's doing everything.

And you're standing in the middle of the thing you wanted, feeling almost nothing, wondering what is wrong with you.

I've written about the chemistry of this. Here's the version that matters at your level, which isn't chemical.

You may have been more attached to the pursuit of love than to love.

That's not a character flaw. It's what happens when pursuit is the only version you've had practice at. Fifteen years of wanting, reaching, working for it, decoding, waiting. That's a skill set and a daily occupation and a source of meaning, and it is completely useless once someone has simply said yes.

So when it arrives, there's a vacancy. The thing that organised your inner life is gone, and what's left is a man and an ordinary Tuesday.

And the ordinary Tuesday feels like a let-down, not because he's disappointing, but because you have never practised receiving. You've only practised wanting.

Here's what I'd want you to consider.

Receiving is a separate skill and almost nobody has it.

It requires tolerating being given to without earning it. It requires letting someone see you on a bad day without managing their response. It requires believing the good thing is yours, which, if you believe you have to earn love, is close to impossible.

So the flatness is often not an absence of feeling. It's a defence against taking it in, and it presents as numbness because taking it in is the frightening option.

How to tell this apart from genuinely not wanting him. Two questions.

Is there anything you can receive? If he does something lovely and you deflect it, minimise it, or immediately do something for him to balance it, that's a receiving problem, not a wanting problem.

And: does the flatness extend to everything good, or just to him? If compliments from anyone bounce off, that's not about this relationship.

What actually works.

Practise receiving in small amounts. When he does something, say thank you and then be quiet. Don't reciprocate, don't deflect, don't make a joke. Just let it land. This is much harder than it sounds and it's the whole exercise.

Let him see something unmanaged. One real thing, not performed. Being received when you're not at your best is the only thing that converts this.

And give it the six weeks of ordinary before deciding. The flatness often lifts once the system stops expecting the loss.

You spent fifteen years getting extremely good at wanting. Nobody taught you the other half, and the other half is the part that actually keeps someone.

Your one move today.

Next time he does something for you, say thank you and then say nothing.

Don't balance it. Just sit in the discomfort of having received something.

Can you receive? Reply honestly, I think most high-achieving women cannot.

See you tomorrow!
